# GospelCO legal and platform research notes

Prepared 20 October 2026 to inform the editable policy drafts. This is a source list and drafting note, not a legal opinion.

## Nigerian privacy

- Nigeria Data Protection Commission, *Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023*: https://ndpc.gov.ng/download/nigeria-data-protection-act-2023
- Nigeria Data Protection Commission, privacy policy and data-handling principles: https://ndpc.gov.ng/our-data-privacy-policy/
- Policy and Legal Advocacy Centre copy of the Act (reference copy): https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Nigeria-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf

Drafting implications: explain the controller/contact, purposes and lawful bases, categories of information, recipients/processors, international transfers, retention, security, rights and how to request access/correction/erasure. The actual notice must match the real deployed stack; update it if analytics, pixels, forms, email lists or client portals are added.

## Nigerian copyright and consumer protection

- Nigerian Copyright Commission, *Copyright Act, 2022* and downloadable Act: https://www.copyright.gov.ng/CopyrightAct/
- Nigerian Copyright Commission announcement regarding the Act: https://copyright.gov.ng/nigeria-gets-new-copyright-act-2022/
- Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission, consumer rights/responsibilities: https://fccpc.gov.ng/consumers/consumer-rights-responsibilities/rights-responsibilities/

Drafting implications: separate ownership/assignment from licences, document rights in writing, distinguish third-party assets from final client deliverables, disclose material prices/terms, and avoid blanket refund language that purports to override mandatory statutory rights. Do not imply that a source credit or non-commercial purpose automatically authorises a copyright use.

## Freelance platforms

- Upwork Legal Center (current Terms of Service, User Agreement, platform policies): https://www.upwork.com/legal
- Fiverr Terms of Service: https://www.fiverr.com/legal-portal/legal-terms/terms-of-service
- Fiverr guidance for responsible AI use by freelancers and clients: https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/37554976380177-Using-AI-on-Fiverr-Guidelines-for-freelancers-and-clients
- Fiverr requirements and guidelines for Gigs/media: https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360011421218-Requirements-and-guidelines-for-your-Gig

Drafting implications: marketplace contracts and rules may govern payment, IP, communication, media rights, refunds and disputes. GospelCO's own general website terms must not be used to bypass platform-specific rules; do not direct marketplace clients off-platform when that is prohibited.

## AI/synthetic content transparency

- YouTube Help, *Disclosing use of GenAI content*: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491
- Meta, *Our Approach to Labeling AI-Generated Content and Manipulated Media*: https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/
- Meta, *Expanding GenAI Transparency for Meta's Ads Products* (updated June 2026): https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/
- Fiverr AI guidance (see above).

Drafting implications: use human review and disclose AI assistance where material or required; use native platform labels for qualifying realistic synthetic content; secure rights/consent for likenesses, voices and inputs; do not promise universal exclusivity/copyright protection for AI outputs.

## Fonts and tools

- Google Fonts for Developers: https://developers.google.com/fonts

Google states that fonts in its catalogue are released under open-source licences and can be used in commercial or non-commercial projects. The exact licence for each font and other resource still controls. Stock services, icon packs, themes, plugins, AI tools, voice/music generators and code libraries may have different plans and restrictions; keep an asset register and re-check the current terms when the project scope or tool changes.

## Important assumptions to confirm before final production

1. Legal contracting name and business-registration details (do not imply incorporation if not confirmed).
2. A monitored privacy/rightsholder contact email (currently set to the legacy GospelCO email in the current source pages).
3. Actual AI vendors, stock/font/music providers and whether client inputs are sent to those vendors.
4. Hosting, Google Fonts, project cart localStorage, analytics/ad pixels, contact forms and embedded widgets actually enabled on the live domain.
5. Whether quotes promise two revisions, source files, full assignment, non-exclusive commercial rights, support periods or specific turnaround times.
6. Whether any client data-processing agreement, confidentiality agreement, image release or talent/voice consent is needed for a given project.
7. Whether terms of a live Upwork/Fiverr/platform job differ from direct GospelCO engagement terms.

A Nigerian lawyer should review the final contracting terms before use for significant commercial engagements, regulated advertising, high-risk synthetic media, disputes, or data processing on behalf of client businesses.
